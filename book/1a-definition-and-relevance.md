## Defining Documentation  

**Documentation** gives relevant details about the exact processes and conditions that were used to create, collect,  process or analyse research objects. **Research objects** are any of the digital or physical materials that are created, collected, used, transformed, or generated during a research or design project. Research objects like these support or underpin research findings. Effective documentation provides **enough relevant information** to make sure that someone (either yourself or others) can **understand, verify and re-use such research objects and reproduce the results**.  

#### An analogy...

:::{card}
Documentation is like creating a recipe and including the chef's notes in the margins; it describes with step-by-step precision how a dish was made from start to finish, and exactly what the chef did to perfect it. This might include which ingredients were used and how much of each, how finely to chop them, in which order to add the ingredients (perhaps with an explanation of why that particular order works best), what temperature settings to cook things at, what happened when the chef undercooked it, in which type of pan, for how long, how much to stir it, and perhaps describe how the dish should look and smell when it's perfectly done. That way, another cook can reproduce the exact dish if they want to. Fellow cooks can understand and appreciate the art that went into making the dish. At the same time, the documented recipe makes it possible for the original chef to retrace their steps and more easily make the dish again. Similarly, when researchers document their workflows and research objects, their **goal** should be to make their **results reproducible and understandable** by others (as well as by their later selves).  
:::


## Relevance 

It is important that you provide documentation for research projects because effective documentation:    

- leads to **better collaboration** because team members can better understand the steps you took and can pick up where you left off.
- results in **less stress and mess** when it's time to write a final report because you have clearly described your process of data collection, processing, and analysis.
- creates **transparency of methods**: documentation helps others to understand exactly how the data was obtained, processed, and analysed.
- allows you to **account for the way in which you carried out** your research project. 
- supports **re-usability** by providing the context needed to understand and correctly use research objects. It explains what was used/created, how it was processed or analysed, and what conditions, tools, or decisions need to be considered when using it again.
- **helps future users** (including your future self) understand the context and the process by which the data were collected.
